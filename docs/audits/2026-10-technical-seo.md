# Audit 2 of 3: Technical SEO

WebScheduler Local (https://local.webscheduler.co.za), audited 17 October 2026 against `main` at `f3d4a5a`.
Report only: no code, data or configuration was changed.

- **Live checks:** GET and HEAD only, one request at a time, 0.3 to 0.4 s apart.
- **Production counts:** read-only `SELECT`s.
- **Search Console:** no exports were provided (`docs/audits/gsc/` doesn't exist), so section 6 of the brief is
  skipped.
- **Performance:** Lighthouse isn't installed. LCP and CLS are lab measurements from Playwright with Chromium on
  emulated mobile (375 px, 4× CPU slowdown, Slow 4G: 150 ms RTT and 1.6 Mbps), as the median of 3 runs. INP needs
  real interaction and **was not measured**. No ranking outcome is promised anywhere in this report.

## 1. Summary

The technical foundations are sound. There is one canonical per page, clean single-hop redirects, real 404s for
missing, unpublished and deleted profiles, `noindex` on search and pagination, rich JSON-LD that matches the
page, and no duplicate titles or descriptions among the 69 indexable pages. The decisive problem is content, not
markup: **all 262 imported profiles (87% of the directory) are `noindex`**, because every one has a description
under 50 characters. Google is offered 34 profiles and a handful of landing pages. Two landing pages (Home Decor
and Clothing & Apparel) and the Gauteng page are indexable while almost every profile they list is not, because
the 3-profile minimum counts profiles that are themselves `noindex`. On performance, CLS is near zero and LCP is
good on most pages, but the cookie banner becomes the LCP element on the province page (5.0 s), and first (cold) hits
take about 1 s against a 0.33 to 0.36 s warm median.

**Redirects:**
- `http://` → `https://`: 301, one hop.
- Trailing slash → no slash: 301.
- The old `listing.` host → `local.`: 301.
- `www.local.` doesn't resolve.
- `/directory/CPT-Dental` answers 200 with the lowercase canonical (SEO-10).

**robots.txt** is generated by `Directory::robots()`. It disallows `/manage`, `/jobs/manage` and `/admin`, and
names the sitemap. It doesn't block `?q=`, which is right: search pages are `noindex, follow`, and blocking them
would stop Google from seeing that.

**Sitemap** (`/sitemap.xml`) is one `urlset` with **81 URLs**:
- 17 static pages, 14 careers pages and 1 vacancy;
- 41 profiles or categories;
- 5 category+province pages, 2 province pages and the Oriental Plaza venue.

Every URL has a `lastmod` taken from data (30 distinct dates). It's built from the same `listing_is_indexable()`
rule the pages use (`DirectoryService.php:1977-1990`), so `noindex` pages are excluded and it updates as profiles
change.

**Internal linking:** a crawl to 3 clicks from `/` (299 pages, no query strings) reached **all 34 indexable
profiles**: 13 at depth 1, 21 at depth 2, and 8 at depth 3. 216 imported profiles sit deeper, all of them
`noindex`. Profiles link to their category, province and venue, and to related businesses.

**Open Graph and Twitter:** every page has `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image` and
`twitter:card=summary_large_image`. Profiles share with their own photo or logo, and only imported profiles with
no image fall back to the site's social image.

## 4. Thin content rule

The current rule (`listing_is_indexable()`) is good and should stay. A profile is indexable only with a
description of at least 50 characters **and** a quality score of at least 40 (unscored passes). It keeps
generated and imported pages out of the index without hiding them from visitors. The recommended rule extends
it to landing pages and adds nothing that generates pages:

1. **Profiles:** indexable when the description is at least 50 plain characters and `quality_score` is at least
   40, as today. Imported profiles follow the same rule; they become indexable when claimed or written up, never
   in bulk.
2. **Category, province and venue pages:** indexable only when they list **at least 3 indexable profiles**
   (today: 3 published profiles of any kind). Page 1 only, as today.
3. **Category + province and future city pages:** indexable at **5 indexable profiles**, because a narrower page
   needs more depth to beat the broader one.
4. **Pagination:** self-canonical and `index, follow` once page 1 qualifies; otherwise `noindex, follow`.
5. **Sitemap:** generated from the same functions as rules 1 to 4, as it already is for rule 1, so the sitemap
   and the meta robots tag can't disagree.

Applied to production today, rule 2 removes `home-decor` and `clothing-apparel` (0 indexable profiles each) from the index.
Gauteng (23 indexable of 287) and Western Cape (8 of 8, all public signups) stay, as do the profiles,
`/directory`, the venue page and the static pages, until the imported profiles are written up.

## 5. Top 5 fixes

1. **SEO-01: write up the imported profiles.** It's the only change that grows the indexable directory from 34
   profiles, and it fits the claim flow in Audit 1.
2. **SEO-02: count indexable profiles for landing pages.** One rule change stops two category pages offering
   Google only `noindex` items.
3. **SEO-03: stop the cookie banner becoming the LCP element.** Every first visit is affected, and that's what
   field data measures.
4. **SEO-04: keep the first hit warm.** Warm TTFB is about 0.35 s; only cold first hits reach 1 s (corrected
   from the first version of this report).
5. **SEO-05: self-canonical pagination.** It keeps deeper profiles discoverable by links as categories grow
   toward 2,500 profiles.

## 6. Out of scope observations

**For the conversion audit**
- The 262 imported profiles are both the indexing gap here and the claim gap there (Audit 1, C-03).
- The "4 provinces" stat sits on a site whose indexable content is Western Cape and Gauteng only.

**For the security audit**
- Every HTML response is `no-store` and sets `ci_session`, so crawler visits create session files (Audit 3,
  S-05).
- The live `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only` header is empty.
- Google Signals and ads beacons are blocked by the CSP (Audit 3, S-11).
